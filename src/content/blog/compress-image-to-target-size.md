Government forms, job portals and exam applications often demand a photo under a strict size — exactly 20KB, 50KB or 100KB. Hitting that target by hand is frustrating. Here's the quick, free way to do it.

## Why file size limits exist

Websites cap upload sizes to save storage and keep pages fast. When a form says "photo must be under 50KB", it will reject anything larger — so you need to shrink the file without making it unreadable.

## Compress to an exact size in seconds

The fastest way is a target-size compressor that does the math for you:

1. Open the tool for your target: [compress to 20KB](/tools/compress-image-to-20kb), [50KB](/tools/compress-image-to-50kb) or [100KB](/tools/compress-image-to-100kb).
2. Drag in your photo.
3. Download the compressed image — it lands right at (or just under) the limit.

Everything runs in your browser, so your photo never leaves your device.

## Prefer to control the quality yourself?

Use the general [image compressor](/tools/compress-image) to dial the quality slider and watch the file size update live. Good for when "smallest possible" matters more than an exact number.

## Tips for the best result

- **Resize first if it's huge.** A 4000px photo squeezed to 20KB looks rough. [Resize it](/tools/resize-image) to a sensible width (say 600–800px) first, then compress.
- **Use JPG for photos.** JPG compresses photos far smaller than PNG. If yours is a PNG, [convert it to JPG](/tools/convert-images) before compressing.
- **Check readability.** For ID photos, make sure the face is still clear after compression.
