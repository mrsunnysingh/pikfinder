PNG is great for logos and screenshots, but the files are large and some websites only accept JPG. Converting between them is simple, free, and takes seconds — no software to install.

## PNG vs JPG — which should you use?

- **JPG** is best for photos. It compresses them into small files, which is why upload forms and email prefer it.
- **PNG** is best for graphics with sharp edges or transparency — logos, icons, screenshots.

If a form rejects your PNG or the file is too big to email, converting to JPG usually solves it. Want the full picture on formats like WebP and AVIF? See our [guide to image formats](/blog/image-formats-explained).

## Convert in three steps

1. **Open the [image converter](/tools/convert-images).**
2. **Drag in your PNG** (or several at once).
3. **Choose JPG and download.** It converts instantly and the file never leaves your device.

The same tool also converts to and from WebP, AVIF and HEIC, so you can turn an iPhone HEIC photo into a universal JPG just as easily.

## Watch out for transparency

PNG supports transparent backgrounds; JPG does not. When you convert a transparent PNG to JPG, the see-through areas turn white. If you need to keep transparency, stay with PNG — or if you want to *remove* a background cleanly, use the free [background remover](/tools/background-remover) first.

## Shrink it further after converting

If the JPG is still too large for an upload limit, run it through the [image compressor](/tools/compress-image) to hit an exact size like 100KB. For photos this usually cuts the file dramatically with no visible difference.

## Tips

- Converting PNG → JPG makes photos much smaller; converting JPG → PNG does *not* improve quality.
- For print, keep the highest quality setting.
- Batch-convert a whole folder at once to save time.
