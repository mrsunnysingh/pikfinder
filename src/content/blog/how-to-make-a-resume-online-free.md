A strong resume gets you the interview. You don't need expensive software or design skills to make one that looks professionally built — you can do it free, in your browser, in a few minutes.

## What every resume needs

Keep it clean and easy to scan — recruiters spend seconds on the first pass:

- **A clear header** with your name, role and contact details.
- **A short summary** — two lines on who you are and what you do.
- **Experience**, most recent first, with results not just duties.
- **Skills and education**, kept tight and relevant to the job.

## Make one in three steps

1. **Pick a template.** Open the free [resume templates](/templates/free-resume-templates) and choose a layout that fits your field.
2. **Fill in your details.** Edit the text, adjust the colours to match your style, and reorder sections to lead with your strength.
3. **Download.** Export a print-ready PDF that keeps its formatting on any device.

## Make it pass the robots

Many companies screen resumes with software before a human sees them. To get through:

- Use standard section headings (Experience, Education, Skills).
- Mirror keywords from the job description where they're honestly true.
- Keep the layout simple — avoid text buried inside images.

## Add a matching cover letter and portfolio

Once your resume looks right, keep the same colours and font across a cover letter using the free [templates library](/templates). If you're in a visual field, build a one-page portfolio or personal header in [Creator Studio](/studio) to stand out.

## Quick tips

- Keep it to one page unless you have 10+ years of experience.
- Save the file as `Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf`.
- Proofread twice — one typo can cost you the callback.
