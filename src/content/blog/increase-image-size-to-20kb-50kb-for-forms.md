Most people fight to make photos *smaller*. But if you've filled out an exam or government form, you may have hit the opposite problem: the portal demands a photo of **at least** a certain size — say "20KB to 50KB" — and rejects your file for being **too small**. Here's how to fix that in seconds, for free.

## Why forms ask for a minimum image size

Portals like SSC, UPSC, banking exams, and many government applications set a size *range* for uploads. The upper limit keeps their servers fast; the lower limit is a rough check that the photo has enough detail to be usable. If your image is, say, 6KB, it falls below the minimum and the form throws an error — even though the photo looks perfectly fine.

Ordinary compressors can't help here: they only make files smaller. You need to go the other way.

## Increase your image to an exact size in three steps

1. **Open the [increase image size tool](/tools/increase-image-size).**
2. **Upload your photo** and enter the target — for example [20KB](/tools/increase-image-size-to-20kb) or [50KB](/tools/increase-image-size-to-50kb).
3. **Download** the resized file. It's now exactly the size you asked for, ready to upload.

Everything runs in your browser, so your photo is never uploaded to a server.

## Does this change how my photo looks?

No — and this is the important part. The tool keeps your pixels and dimensions exactly the same. It reaches the target size by adding a small amount of standards-compliant filler data (a JPEG comment segment) that every image viewer simply ignores. The photo looks identical; only the file's byte size grows to meet the minimum. The result is still a valid JPEG that opens everywhere and passes upload checks.

## Hitting an exact range (e.g. "20KB to 50KB")

Forms often specify a band, not a single number. That's easy:

- If your photo is **too small**, use the [increase image size tool](/tools/increase-image-size) to bring it up to the minimum (or anywhere inside the range).
- If your photo is **too large**, use the [image compressor](/tools/compress-image) to bring it down under the maximum.

Between the two, you can land any file at any size the form wants.

## Tips for form photos

- **Check the exact requirement first** — size range, dimensions (often 200×230 px for photos), and format (usually JPG).
- **Set the size to the middle of the range** to be safe, not right at the edge.
- **Keep the original** in case a different form needs a different size.
- **Use JPG**, which is what almost every portal expects.
