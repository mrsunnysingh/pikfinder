Every photo you take carries a hidden layer of data you never see. Before you upload a picture to a marketplace, a forum, or a dating profile, it's worth knowing what that data reveals — and how to remove it in a few seconds, for free.

## What is photo metadata?

When your phone or camera saves an image, it also writes a block of information called **EXIF** (Exchangeable Image File Format) into the file. This can include:

- **GPS coordinates** — the exact spot where the photo was taken, often accurate to a few metres.
- **Date and time** — down to the second.
- **Device details** — your camera or phone make and model.
- **Camera settings** — shutter speed, aperture, ISO, and sometimes editing software or an owner name.

None of this is visible in the picture itself, but anyone who downloads the file can read it with a free tool.

## Why it matters for your privacy

The GPS tag is the real concern. A single photo posted online can quietly reveal your home address, your child's school, or where you work. Most big social networks strip metadata on upload, but many places don't — marketplace listings, classified ads, forums, direct file transfers, email attachments, and personal websites often keep the original file intact.

If you'd like to see exactly what's hiding in one of your own photos, drop it into the free [EXIF viewer](/tools/exif-viewer) — it lists every embedded field, including any location, in a readable table.

## A quick habit before you post

- **Selling something online?** Strip metadata from product photos so buyers can't see where you live.
- **Sharing photos of kids?** Remove the location tag before it goes anywhere public.
